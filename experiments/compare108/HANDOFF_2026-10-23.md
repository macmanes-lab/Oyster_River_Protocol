## 2. Job 1325632: merge-loss trace (Matt's priority)

**Question.** Matt's position is that ORP should never be beaten by one of its own
components. Section 4.3 currently reports six runs where a component matches or beats ORP
on BUSCO completeness, and two where Trans-ABySS has more unique genes. The six:
- *Nicotiana* (orp_SRR3499127)
- *Avena* (tsa_GAJE)
- GAGE
- *Echinogammarus* (tsa_GARO)
- *Kerria* (tsa_GBDO, tie)
- *Heterorhabditis* (orp_ERR1016675, tie)

The two gene cases are *Patiria* (tsa_GAWB) and *Anopheles* (orp_ERR489297). On BUSCO
**missing**, the best single component beats ORP in 21 of 59 runs.

**Scripts** (new, in compare108):
- `trace_merge_losses.py`, `trace_merge_losses.sbatch`: array 1-59 over
  `lineage_410/manifest.tsv`; outputs in `lineage_410/merge_trace/<SRR>/`.

**What the trace does.** For every BUSCO complete in some component but F/M in ORP, and every
Swiss-Prot gene hit in a component but not in ORP, it records:
- the component contig;
- the contig's best DIAMOND hit;
- how many ORP contigs have that gene as best hit;
- the best match in the ORP assembly (minimap2 or blastn if the orp env has one);
- which run files mention the contig.

**Cause labels.**
- `nohit_path`: the contig has no Swiss-Prot hit, so it went through CD-HIT and `--min-cov`.
- `copy_selection`: the gene is in ORP under other contigs.
- `gene_dropped`: no ORP contig for the gene at all.
- `seq_present`: the sequence is in ORP at ≥95% identity and ≥90% coverage, but BUSCO
  scored it differently.
- For lost genes: `secondary_only` (only ever a non-best hit within `--top 0.1`) or
  `best_hit_dropped`.

**DIAMOND.** The trace reruns DIAMOND on all 5 assemblies exactly as ORP does (`blastx -e 1e-8
--top 0.1`, `software/diamond/swissprot`), cached with a `.done` marker. It then checks the
recomputed unique-gene counts against each qualreport.

  runs (SRR389086, SRR389085), the largest assemblies, so OOM is suspected but unconfirmed.
- Fix written: DIAMOND log kept, real error raised, empty-hit case handled, `--tmpdir`, and
  the sbatch raised to 32 cpus / 128G.

**Preliminary results (20 of the 26 finished runs, from the log lines).**
- **BUSCO losses are everywhere.** Every run lost some: 1-38 BUSCOs complete in a component but
  F/M in ORP, usually 9-20. Mostly `copy_selection` (≈60-100% per run). `gene_dropped` is 0-1
  per run. One run is 33/38 `nohit_path`; find out which (probably GAGE or another poorly
  annotated taxon).
- **Gene losses are large.** 207-1,402 genes per run are hit in a component but absent from
  ORP. Only ~10% are `secondary_only`, so most were a component contig's *best* hit. If the
  merge keeps one representative per best-hit gene, that should not happen, which suggests
  contigs are removed **before** grouping. Candidates:
  - a length or expression prefilter in `pool_branch`;
  - a stricter DIAMOND or coverage criterion inside the merge;
  - representative selection requiring hit coverage.
  
  Confirm with the `in_orp` and `merge_files` columns after collate, then read
  `twotrack_select.py` and the pool step in the repo.
- **Gene counts.** 5 of 20 runs report `gene counts match qualreport: False`. Check which labels
  disagree (`unique_genes_recomputed_*` vs `unique_genes_qualreport_*` in the summary csv)
  before trusting those runs' gene numbers. ORP's own count may come from its earlier hit
  lookups rather than a fresh search.

**Known limitation.** `merge_files` uses `grep -F`, so an id like `..._i1` also matches
`..._i10`. Treat it as a lead, not proof.
